# Beachcar Build Week video storyboard

Working title: **Your Codex. Your taste. Your car.**

Category: **Apps for Your Life**

Target runtime: **about 1:41, set by the final Chris narration**

Export ceiling: **2:10**

## Editorial promise

Begin with a small, specific family problem, not a product claim. Let the viewer imagine the unnamed beach car, show Codex hunting from the Mac, and withhold the complete Surrey until the midpoint. Only then does the ridiculous pink car fill the frame and the owner says he bought it.

The viewer should remember the one-to-two-mile family errands, the delayed Surrey reveal, **this one could ruin my summer**, and the fact that the user stays in control.

## Visual rules

- Frame: 16:9, 1920x1080, 30 fps. Keep essential type inside a 10% title-safe margin.
- Opening lockup, exactly: `BEACHCAR` / `your codex - your taste - your car`.
- Palette: Surrey pink `#ef5b91`, blush `#f6d7df`, paper `#f7f2e8`, and ink `#080a0b`. No fluorescent green.
- Car details first; complete car later. No moon, starscape, faux-chrome fantasy, or decorative setting that competes with it.
- Use the real purchased 1960 Willys Surrey throughout. Do not use generated or synthetic car imagery.
- Use hard cuts, gentle 1-3% moves on stills, and details from the real gallery to create rhythm. Avoid template transitions and a rapid slideshow.
- Captions are always on. Use original narration and interface sounds; add music only if its publication license is documented.
- Never expose passwords, tokens, email addresses, notifications, browser profiles, account menus, or private listing comments.

## Final editorial gates

- Runtime stays below 2:10, with enough time to enjoy the car and its reveal.
- Sections one and two show only details; the complete Surrey and purchase reveal land together in section four.
- Section three uses clean taste, calibration, shortlist, and opinion footage rather than a setup tutorial.
- Deterministic result claims, the desktop handoff/dummy-email capture, and technical product-deck graphics stay out of the general YouTube cut; the privacy-scrubbed working navigation replay is allowed and labeled.
- Nonspoken submission notes separate the earlier Surrey discovery from the Build Week work.
- The Garage Session is unmistakably opt-in and stops for user review before anything ships.
- No generated car imagery appears anywhere, including inside phone or Mac captures.
- Every photo, sound, font, and visible third-party mark maps to the permissions log.
- The upload works with narration, captions, or both, and ends on **Apps for Your Life**.
